# Dragon Ball Lore Guide

> **Scope note:** Dragon Ball is a long-running franchise with manga, anime, films, games, spin-offs, and alternate timelines. No single README can contain *all* of its lore, and those continuities sometimes differ. This is an original, spoiler-heavy overview of the main story and its key ideas—not a substitute for the works themselves. The manga and the *Dragon Ball Z* anime broadly tell the same central story, while *Dragon Ball GT*, many films, and game stories follow separate or alternate continuities. *Dragon Ball Super* continues the story in its own manga and anime versions, which also have differences.

## At a glance

**Dragon Ball** began as Akira Toriyama's manga about Son Goku, a strong-tailed boy with a mysterious past, and Bulma, an inventor searching for seven magical Dragon Balls. Their early adventure grows into a martial-arts comedy, then an epic about alien heritage, world-threatening enemies, gods, and the many universes. Across every era, the core themes are adventure, friendship, self-improvement, mercy, family, and finding another challenge after overcoming the last one.

## The central story

### 1. The first Dragon Ball quest

Bulma meets Goku in the wilderness and learns he owns a four-star Dragon Ball, a keepsake from his grandfather, Son Gohan. The Dragon Balls are seven crystal spheres that summon the dragon Shenron when gathered; Shenron can grant a wish, subject to limits that change as the story progresses. Bulma and Goku travel the world, meeting allies and rivals—including Yamcha, Oolong, and the Ox-King's daughter Chi-Chi—and repeatedly crossing paths with the scheming Emperor Pilaf.

Goku trains under Master Roshi alongside Krillin. The World Martial Arts Tournament introduces a recurring pattern: combat is a test of discipline and character, not just a way to defeat villains. Goku also confronts the Red Ribbon Army, a military organization seeking the Dragon Balls, and discovers that his strength can bring him into conflict with much larger forces. He later trains with Korin and Kami, Earth's guardian, and faces the demon king Piccolo. Piccolo's defeat leads to the arrival of his reincarnated counterpart, who becomes a rival and eventually one of Goku's most important allies.

### 2. Saiyans and the Namek conflict

At the start of the *Z*-era story, Goku learns that he is a Saiyan—an alien warrior race—and that his birth name is Kakarot. Raditz, Goku's brother, reveals that Goku was sent to Earth as an infant. Goku and Piccolo cooperate to stop Raditz, and Goku sacrifices himself. The surviving Saiyans, Vegeta and Nappa, then head for Earth. Goku returns after training with King Kai; the battles cost many lives, and the heroes use Earth's Dragon Balls to revive their friends.

The search for a way to restore those lost in the Saiyan battle takes the heroes to Namek, home of the Namekians and their own Dragon Balls. There they collide with Frieza, an imperial tyrant who has conquered worlds and fears the emergence of a Super Saiyan. Vegeta's pursuit of power, the heroes' alliances with Namekians, and Goku's eventual transformation make the conflict a turning point. Frieza is defeated, Namek is destroyed, and the survivors escape through a series of desperate rescues and wishes.

### 3. Androids, Cell, and the next generation

A young time traveler named Trunks arrives from a devastated future and warns that artificial humans—Androids 17 and 18—will destroy his world. His warning changes history but does not prevent a new crisis. Dr. Gero's creations and the bio-engineered Cell drive the heroes into a battle over strength, responsibility, and passing the future to the next generation.

Goku's son Gohan, who has repeatedly shown extraordinary power, becomes central to the Cell Games. Goku entrusts the fight to Gohan, who surpasses his own limits and defeats Cell. Goku dies again, this time choosing to remain in the afterlife because he believes his presence attracts danger to Earth. Gohan grows up, while the other fighters continue their lives.

### 4. Majin Buu and the end of the original manga story

Years later, Gohan is a student and a masked superhero called the Great Saiyaman. The wizard Babidi seeks to awaken Majin Buu, a being whose form and behavior shift through absorption and transformation. Vegeta's pride and desire to test himself make him vulnerable to Babidi's influence, but his decision to protect his family reveals how far he has changed.

Buu's rampage pushes the heroes to use new fusions and transformations. Goku and Vegeta eventually work together, and Earth's people help create the energy for the decisive Spirit Bomb. After Buu is defeated, Goku hopes to meet and train the reincarnation of the kind-hearted part of Buu: Uub. Their meeting at the World Martial Arts Tournament closes the manga's main story with the promise of another adventure.

### 5. *Dragon Ball Super*: gods and the multiverse

*Dragon Ball Super* takes place after the defeat of Buu and before Goku's final meeting with Uub. Beerus, the God of Destruction for Universe 7, awakens and searches for the Super Saiyan God. His angel attendant Whis becomes a mentor to Goku and Vegeta. The story expands the setting from Earth and the afterlife to a multiverse of twelve remaining universes, each with a God of Destruction, an Angel attendant, and a Supreme Kai.

Major story arcs include the contest with the resurrected Frieza; a tournament between Universe 6 and Universe 7; the Goku Black and Future Trunks crisis; the Tournament of Power, in which teams fight for their universes' survival; and conflicts involving the Galactic Patrol and powerful survivors such as Moro and Granolah in the manga. The Tournament of Power also introduces Jiren and gives Android 17 a pivotal role. The Super Dragon Balls—enormous spheres spread across two universes—can grant wishes on a vastly greater scale than Earth's or Namek's Dragon Balls.

The *Super* anime and manga are related but not identical adaptations: details, fights, and the order or treatment of events can differ. The later films *Dragon Ball Super: Broly* and *Dragon Ball Super: Super Hero* add major events to the modern continuity, including Broly's introduction and a new crisis involving the Red Ribbon Army.

## Major characters

- **Son Goku / Kakarot:** Saiyan raised on Earth. Cheerful, curious, and driven by the joy of testing himself; he often gives enemies a chance to change.
- **Bulma:** Brilliant inventor and one of Goku's first companions. Her technology and initiative repeatedly make the adventures possible.
- **Krillin:** Goku's longtime friend and fellow martial artist. Courage and loyalty matter more than being the strongest.
- **Piccolo:** Begins as a dangerous enemy and becomes a stern mentor and protector, especially to Gohan.
- **Son Gohan:** Goku and Chi-Chi's son. A gentle scholar whose enormous potential emerges when people he loves are threatened.
- **Vegeta:** Prince of the Saiyans. His rivalry with Goku becomes a journey from conquest and pride to family, responsibility, and chosen loyalty.
- **Chi-Chi:** Goku's wife and Gohan and Goten's mother; strong-willed and determined that her children receive an education.
- **Trunks and Goten:** The next generation of Saiyans. Trunks has a future-born counterpart whose warnings reshape the story.
- **Androids 17 and 18:** Humans transformed by Dr. Gero. Initially opponents, they ultimately choose their own lives and become allies.
- **Master Roshi:** Goku and Krillin's eccentric martial-arts teacher, whose training emphasizes fundamentals and persistence.
- **Frieza:** A cruel galactic emperor whose empire and fear of Saiyan power shape much of the Saiyan and Namek story.
- **Beerus and Whis:** Universe 7's God of Destruction and Angel attendant. They introduce divine hierarchies and a new level of training.
- **Shenron, Porunga, and other dragons:** Wish-granting dragons linked to different sets of Dragon Balls, each with distinct rules and makers.

## How the world works

### Dragon Balls and wishes

Dragon Balls are created by particular beings, and their rules depend on their creator. Earth's set is tied to its guardian; Namek has its own set and dragon, Porunga; and the Super Dragon Balls span Universes 6 and 7. A dragon's power is not unlimited: wishes have restrictions, and a set may become inert or change after a wish until it is restored. The series revisits the consequences of relying on wishes, including who can be revived and how often.

### Ki and martial arts

**Ki** is life energy that trained fighters can sense, control, strengthen, and project. Techniques include the Kamehameha, a focused energy wave; the Spirit Bomb, which gathers energy offered by living beings and nature; and the Instant Transmission, which lets Goku travel to a sensed energy signature. Training, strategy, stamina, and self-control matter alongside raw power.

### Saiyans and transformations

Saiyans are a warrior people whose history includes Planet Vegeta, a moon-related Great Ape transformation for those with tails, and the legendary Super Saiyan. The story introduces many later Saiyan forms, but transformations are not a complete measure of character or skill. Goku, Vegeta, Gohan, Trunks, and others develop in different ways; their forms and exact naming can vary by continuity and adaptation.

### Afterlife, gods, and universes

The setting includes the living world, the afterlife, and divine realms. King Yemma judges the dead; King Kai trains selected fighters; Supreme Kais oversee creation and cosmic balance; Gods of Destruction destroy worlds when necessary; and Angels serve and guide Gods of Destruction. In *Dragon Ball Super*, Zeno rules over the multiverse and can erase universes. These roles and the cosmic hierarchy are expanded over time.

### Fusions

Fusion combines fighters into a more powerful being. The Metamoran Fusion Dance requires synchronized poses and produces a temporary fusion; the Potara earrings provide another method, with rules that receive further explanation in *Super*. Fusions such as Gotenks and Vegito are distinct characters with their own personalities and time limits or conditions.

## Continuities and adaptations

The term “canon” can be confusing because Dragon Ball has multiple versions:

- **Original manga:** Toriyama's *Dragon Ball* manga is the foundation, continuing from Goku's childhood through the Buu story and Uub.
- **Classic anime:** *Dragon Ball* and *Dragon Ball Z* adapt the manga, with anime-only episodes and differences. *Dragon Ball Z Kai* is a later, more manga-focused edit of *Z*.
- **Dragon Ball Super:** The TV anime, manga, and films share a broad era and core ideas but do not match in every detail.
- **Dragon Ball GT:** A sequel anime produced after *Z*, with its own story and continuity, rather than an adaptation of Toriyama's original manga sequel.
- **Films, specials, games, and spin-offs:** Many tell alternate or self-contained stories. Older films often do not fit neatly into the main timeline; games may intentionally combine characters and eras.

When comparing events, identify the specific work or continuity rather than assuming every version is one perfectly unified timeline.

## Themes and recurring ideas

1. **Growth has no finish line:** Goku and his friends keep learning after every victory.
2. **Rivals can change:** Piccolo and Vegeta show that a person's choices can matter more than their origins.
3. **Strength carries responsibility:** The heroes' power creates a duty to protect others and think about consequences.
4. **Mercy is a risk, not a guarantee:** Goku often spares enemies, sometimes with surprising results; compassion does not erase the danger of a choice.
5. **The next generation matters:** Gohan, Trunks, Goten, and Uub inherit a world shaped by earlier choices.
6. **Adventure and humor remain central:** Even as the stakes grow cosmic, the series keeps its sense of play, absurdity, and wonder.

## Quick glossary

- **Capsule Corporation:** Bulma's family company, known for technology that stores objects in compact capsules.
- **Great Ape / Ōzaru:** A giant ape form associated with Saiyans who have tails and can see a full moon.
- **Kamehameha:** Master Roshi's signature energy-wave technique, later learned by several students.
- **Kaioken:** A high-risk technique taught by King Kai that temporarily amplifies a fighter's power.
- **Namekian:** A species including Piccolo and the Dragon Clan members who create Dragon Balls.
- **Potara:** Divine earrings used for fusion.
- **Saiyan:** The warrior species to which Goku and Vegeta belong.
- **Super Saiyan:** A legendary Saiyan transformation first reached by Goku during the Namek conflict.
- **World Martial Arts Tournament:** A recurring competition that brings fighters together and marks several stages of Goku's life.

## Further reading and viewing

For the most direct route through the central story, read the original manga from the beginning through the Uub epilogue, or watch *Dragon Ball* followed by *Dragon Ball Z* or *Dragon Ball Z Kai*. Then explore *Dragon Ball Super* and its manga or films, keeping their differences in mind. *Dragon Ball GT*, movies, specials, and games are worth exploring as separate branches of the franchise.
