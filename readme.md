# Dragon Ball Lore & Technique Compendium

This guide gives a broad, organized account of the main story and a substantial catalog of notable techniques. Technique visuals are described in original, high-level language rather than copied from scripts or frame-by-frame recreations. Names, effects, and continuity can vary by translation and version.

## Series overview

**Dragon Ball**, created by Akira Toriyama, begins as a comic adventure about Son Goku, a powerful, good-hearted boy with a tail, and Bulma, an inventor looking for seven magical Dragon Balls. The search introduces martial-arts masters, eccentric companions, dangerous armies, and wish-granting dragons. Over time, the story grows from tournament comedy into a saga about alien ancestry, family, redemption, cosmic powers, and the many universes.

At its heart, the series is about growth. Training matters, but so do compassion, ingenuity, friendship, and knowing when to rely on others. Goku is motivated by the challenge of becoming stronger; his friends and rivals give that pursuit meaning and help protect the people and worlds he might otherwise leave behind.

## Story timeline

### The early adventures and tournaments

Bulma meets Goku in the wilderness and learns he has the four-star Dragon Ball, a keepsake from his grandfather, Son Gohan. The two travel to find the seven spheres, which summon Shenron and allow a wish within the dragon's limits. They encounter Yamcha, Oolong, the Ox-King and Chi-Chi, and the would-be conqueror Emperor Pilaf. Goku's first adventures blend slapstick, exploration, folklore, and martial arts.

Goku and Krillin train with Master Roshi, whose lessons stress practical conditioning and humility. At the World Martial Arts Tournament, Goku meets formidable opponents and learns that winning is not the only measure of a fighter. Later conflicts with the Red Ribbon Army, the assassin Tao Pai Pai, and the demon king Piccolo push him into more dangerous battles. Goku trains with Korin and Kami, Earth's guardian; after Piccolo's defeat, Piccolo's reincarnation grows into a rival and eventually a trusted protector.

### Saiyans and Namek

Raditz arrives on Earth and reveals that Goku is a Saiyan named Kakarot, sent away as an infant. Goku and Piccolo join forces against him. Vegeta and Nappa follow, and the battle costs several heroes their lives. Goku returns after training with King Kai, and the survivors use Earth's Dragon Balls to restore their friends.

The search for Namek's Dragon Balls brings the heroes into conflict with Frieza, a tyrant whose empire has subjugated countless worlds. Vegeta's shifting alliances, the Namekians' resistance, and Goku's arrival turn the struggle into a contest of survival and identity. Frieza's cruelty triggers Goku's first Super Saiyan transformation. Namek is destroyed, but the survivors escape through wishes and rescues.

### Androids, Cell, and Gohan

Future Trunks warns that artificial humans created by Dr. Gero will devastate his timeline. The warning changes events without eliminating the threat. Androids 17 and 18, the energy-absorbing 19 and 20, and the bio-engineered Cell force the heroes to confront a foe who grows stronger by absorbing others.

During the Cell Games, Goku recognizes that Gohan can surpass him and entrusts the decisive fight to his son. Gohan's anger and grief unlock a new level of power; he defeats Cell with help from his father and friends. Goku gives his life to prevent another catastrophe and remains in the afterlife, believing Earth may be safer without him.

### Majin Buu and the original manga's conclusion

Years later, Gohan balances school with his Great Saiyaman identity. The wizard Babidi seeks to awaken Majin Buu, a being whose form and temperament change as he is hurt, split, or absorbs others. Vegeta allows Babidi's influence to reach him so he can fight Goku at full strength, but his love for his family leads him to sacrifice himself in an attempt to stop Buu.

The heroes use fusion, new transformations, and teamwork as Buu threatens Earth and the afterlife. Goku and Vegeta eventually work together; the people of Earth contribute energy to the Spirit Bomb that defeats Kid Buu. At the story's close, Goku meets Uub, the reincarnation of Buu's good side, and leaves to train him.

### *Dragon Ball Super*: gods and the multiverse

Set before Goku's meeting with Uub, *Dragon Ball Super* introduces Beerus, Universe 7's God of Destruction, and Whis, his Angel attendant. Their arrival leads to the Super Saiyan God ritual and a wider cosmic setting. Major events include the return of Frieza, a tournament between Universes 6 and 7, the Goku Black/Future Trunks crisis, and the Tournament of Power, where teams fight to save their universes. The manga continues with conflicts involving the Galactic Patrol, Moro, and Granolah. The films *Dragon Ball Super: Broly* and *Super Hero* add Broly's story and a new Red Ribbon Army crisis.

The *Super* manga and anime tell related but not identical versions; some fights, details, and developments differ. The *Super* films also have their own presentation and should be considered when following chronology.

## World, power, and transformations

### Dragon Balls and wishes

Dragon Balls are made by particular creators, and each set has its own dragon and rules. Earth's set summons Shenron; Namek's summons Porunga; and the immense Super Dragon Balls summon Super Shenron. Wishes have limits, may require particular conditions, and can affect when a set becomes usable again. The story repeatedly shows that resurrection cannot simply erase every consequence.

### Ki and combat

**Ki** is life energy. Fighters learn to sense it, conceal it, focus it, and release it as blasts or barriers. A technique's effect depends on timing, control, stamina, and the user's condition—not simply the size of its animation. Some skills are learned through practice, others are personal innovations, and still others are temporary power amplifiers with serious physical costs.

### Saiyans and forms

Saiyans are a warrior people whose history includes Planet Vegeta and the Great Ape form, traditionally triggered by a tailed Saiyan seeing a full moon or an equivalent source of Blutz Waves. Super Saiyan and later forms mark different paths of development. Transformations can change strength, speed, appearance, and energy use, but the series' many continuities do not always use identical labels or rules. Goku, Vegeta, Gohan, Trunks, Broly, and others reach power in distinct ways.

### The afterlife and divine hierarchy

The setting includes the living world, the afterlife, and divine realms. King Yemma judges the dead; King Kai trains selected fighters; Supreme Kais oversee creation; Gods of Destruction maintain cosmic balance through destruction; and Angels guide their assigned Gods of Destruction. Zeno rules over the multiverse and can erase universes. This hierarchy expands as the story progresses.

## Technique guide

### How to read the technique entries

Each entry gives a common name, typical user(s), what the move does, and a **visual shorthand**—a high-level description of the kind of motion or effect often depicted in animation. It is not a frame-accurate description; choreography changes between manga panels, anime episodes, films, and games. Some attacks have several translated names, and many characters use generic ki blasts that are not formally named.

### Foundational martial-arts techniques

- **Kamehameha — Master Roshi, Goku, Krillin, Gohan, and others.** A concentrated ki wave fired from the hands after gathering energy. **Visual shorthand:** the user cups their hands, draws them back, calls out the move, then thrusts forward as a bright beam surges toward the target. It can be adjusted in size and direction; Goku's aerial and one-handed variations change the setup.
- **Power Pole — Goku.** A magical staff used for striking, vaulting, and extending reach. It is a physical weapon rather than a ki beam. **Visual shorthand:** quick staff combinations and sudden extensions turn a short-range exchange into a long-range strike.
- **Afterimage Technique — Roshi, Goku, and other martial artists.** A burst of speed leaves a misleading image, allowing the user to evade or reposition. **Visual shorthand:** the opponent attacks an apparent silhouette while the real fighter is already at another angle.
- **Drunken Fist — Master Roshi (as Jackie Chun).** An intentionally erratic style that disguises timing and direction. **Visual shorthand:** swaying, off-balance movements abruptly become precise counters. It is a comic martial-arts trick, not a literal power-up.
- **Wolf Fang Fist — Yamcha.** A rapid claw-like hand-strike combination inspired by a wolf. **Visual shorthand:** low, rushing footwork leads into a flurry of alternating swipes and a finishing blow.
- **Thunder Shock Surprise — Master Roshi.** A restraint technique that traps a target in an electrical-looking field. **Visual shorthand:** the target is held in place while crackling energy surrounds them. It is used for control, not as a standard projectile.
- **Solar Flare (Taiyōken) — Tien, Krillin, Goku, and others.** A sudden flash blinds nearby opponents. **Visual shorthand:** the user raises their hands and a screen-filling burst of light interrupts the fight, creating an opening to escape or attack.
- **Evil Containment Wave (Mafūba) — Roshi, Mutaito, Tien, and others.** A dangerous sealing technique that funnels a target into a prepared container. **Visual shorthand:** spiraling energy lifts and draws the target toward a jar or vessel. It takes precision and can endanger the user if the seal fails.

### Goku and Saiyan techniques

- **Kaioken — Goku.** King Kai's technique multiplies output for a short time, placing severe strain on the body. **Visual shorthand:** a red aura flares around Goku as his movements and attacks intensify. Higher multipliers are more dangerous; it is a technique layered over a state, not a permanent transformation.
- **Spirit Bomb (Genki-dama) — Goku.** Gathers life energy offered by surrounding beings and nature into a sphere. **Visual shorthand:** Goku raises his arms while a growing orb forms overhead, then hurls it. The charge can take time, and success depends on gathering energy and the target's vulnerability; it is not simply Goku's own ki.
- **Instant Transmission (Shunkan Idō) — Goku.** Teleports to a ki signature he can sense, often using two fingers at his forehead to concentrate. **Visual shorthand:** Goku vanishes and reappears beside an ally, behind an enemy, or at a distant location. It is a movement skill, not an attack by itself, though he can combine it with strikes or beams.
- **Dragon Fist — Goku (notably in *GT* and some games/films).** A dramatic close-range finisher often depicted as a dragon-shaped energy effect bursting through the opponent. **Visual shorthand:** Goku surges forward with a powerful strike as a golden dragon-like aura traces the attack. Its continuity and presentation vary; it is not a standard manga technique.
- **Super Saiyan transformations — Saiyans.** Transformations increase combat capability and alter the user's aura and appearance. **Visual shorthand:** a surge of ki, a change in hair or aura, and a sharp change in the pace of the fight. Each form has different costs and strengths; transformations are states, not named attacks.
- **Meteor Combination — Goku.** A sequence of fast martial-arts strikes, commonly associated with the early tournament era. **Visual shorthand:** a rush of punches and kicks drives the opponent through the air before a final hit sends them away. Exact choreography varies.
- **Saiyan-style ki volley — Goku, Gohan, and many Saiyans.** Repeated compact blasts pressure an opponent or limit their movement. **Visual shorthand:** rapid hand motions launch a cluster of small energy shots, followed by a larger finishing blast. This is a broad attack pattern rather than one unique technique.

### Vegeta's signature techniques

- **Galick Gun — Vegeta.** A powerful beam attack, first used against Goku. **Visual shorthand:** Vegeta braces with both hands near his side, gathers energy, and drives a violet beam forward. It is often presented as a direct beam contest with the Kamehameha.
- **Big Bang Attack — Vegeta.** A concentrated blast released from an outstretched hand. **Visual shorthand:** Vegeta extends his palm and fires a bright, compact sphere that expands as it travels or impacts. It is more focused than an uncontrolled volley.
- **Final Flash — Vegeta.** A massive, high-output beam requiring a broad, deliberate charge. **Visual shorthand:** Vegeta spreads his arms to gather energy, brings them forward, and unleashes a wide golden beam. It trades a conspicuous wind-up for enormous force.
- **Final Explosion — Vegeta.** A self-sacrificial release of energy in a wide blast. **Visual shorthand:** Vegeta's aura swells, then bursts outward in a sphere that engulfs the surrounding area. Its defining feature is the user's willingness to risk their life.
- **Galick Blaster and named game variants — Vegeta and game depictions.** Games and adaptations add compound names and altered versions of Vegeta's beams. **Visual shorthand:** usually a charged beam or a beam chained into close-range attacks. Exact names and effects are continuity-specific.
- **Energy rings / restraint — Vegeta in some adaptations.** Ki can be shaped into bands that pin or restrict a target. **Visual shorthand:** glowing loops close around the opponent, limiting movement before a follow-up attack. Availability and naming vary by version.

### Piccolo, Namekian, and regeneration techniques

- **Special Beam Cannon (Makankōsappō) — Piccolo.** A piercing, focused beam developed to defeat Raditz. **Visual shorthand:** Piccolo charges with two fingers at his forehead, then fires a thin spiral beam, sometimes accompanied by a second, wider energy trail. It concentrates force along a narrow path.
- **Hellzone Grenade — Piccolo.** A field of suspended ki spheres that converge on a target. **Visual shorthand:** Piccolo launches multiple glowing orbs around an opponent, pauses, then directs them inward in a coordinated barrage.
- **Light Grenade — Piccolo.** A large energy attack formed between the hands. **Visual shorthand:** Piccolo raises and spreads his arms as a bright sphere gathers, then releases it as an explosive wave.
- **Explosive Demon Wave — Piccolo and related Namekian fighters.** A broad energy blast released from the hands. **Visual shorthand:** a compact charge becomes a forceful, forward-facing burst. Names and depictions can vary across translations.
- **Regeneration — Piccolo and other Namekians.** A biological ability that can restore damaged body parts, provided the user has enough energy and the damage is survivable. **Visual shorthand:** a severed limb or wound visibly reforms. It is an ability, not an attack, and costs stamina.
- **Giant Form — Piccolo.** Piccolo enlarges his body for reach and intimidation. **Visual shorthand:** his frame expands dramatically before he uses long-range grabs and heavy strikes. Size alone does not guarantee greater speed or victory.
- **Stretching limbs — Namekians.** Namekian bodies can extend their arms to strike or seize distant targets. **Visual shorthand:** an arm shoots across the battlefield for a sudden punch, block, or grab.

### Earthling and other allies' techniques

- **Destructo Disc (Kienzan) — Krillin, Goku, and others.** A thin, spinning ki blade designed to cut through much tougher defenses. **Visual shorthand:** a flat disc forms above the hand and is thrown along a curving or direct path. It is dangerous but can be dodged; it does not automatically hit or defeat every opponent.
- **Tri-Beam (Kikōhō) — Tien.** A powerful technique that converts the user's life energy into repeated blasts. **Visual shorthand:** Tien forms a triangle with his hands and fires a square-edged energy column into the ground or target. It can hold back a stronger foe at severe physical cost.
- **Neo Tri-Beam — Tien.** A repeated, intensified application of the Tri-Beam. **Visual shorthand:** Tien fires successive blasts while bracing himself, each shot straining his body. It shows resolve, not a sustainable fighting strategy.
- **Dodon's Ray — Tien, Chiaotzu, and Crane School fighters.** A narrow fingertip energy shot. **Visual shorthand:** a finger points and a compact beam snaps forward with little wind-up.
- **Chiaotzu's telekinesis and psychic attacks — Chiaotzu.** Mental abilities can restrain or disrupt an opponent. **Visual shorthand:** the target appears immobilized or is forced off balance while Chiaotzu concentrates. Strong opponents can resist, and the technique leaves him vulnerable.
- **Destructo Disc variants — Krillin and adaptations.** The disc can be split, curved, or used in combinations in some versions. **Visual shorthand:** multiple spinning blades approach from different angles. Specific variants are not identical across anime and games.
- **Hakai — Gods of Destruction and trained users in some continuities.** Destruction energy can erase a target rather than merely injure it. **Visual shorthand:** destructive energy envelops the target and removes or disintegrates them. Depiction, limits, and who can use it differ between manga and anime.
- **Android energy absorption — Androids 19 and 20; 17 and 18 have different energy systems.** Certain models absorb incoming energy through devices in their palms. **Visual shorthand:** an energy blast is caught or drained rather than exploding on impact. The ability is model-specific, not shared by every Android.

### Villain and rival techniques

- **Death Beam — Frieza.** A fast, narrow beam fired from a fingertip. **Visual shorthand:** Frieza points, and a thin shot crosses the space with little charging time. Its speed and precision make it dangerous even when visually small.
- **Death Ball — Frieza and related forms.** A destructive sphere formed in one hand and launched or driven toward a target. **Visual shorthand:** a dark, glowing orb grows above the fingertip before being hurled, sometimes toward a planet.
- **Nova Strike — Frieza (name commonly used in games).** A high-speed charge wrapped in energy. **Visual shorthand:** Frieza becomes a blazing projectile and rams through the opponent. Naming and exact effects vary by adaptation.
- **Crusher Ball — Jeice.** A ball of ki thrown with a distinctive sidearm motion. **Visual shorthand:** Jeice swings his arm and sends a compact orb on a curving path.
- **Recoome Eraser Gun / Recoome Boom — Recoome and game versions.** Recoome combines showmanship with heavy energy attacks. **Visual shorthand:** a pose or taunt transitions into a sudden blast or explosive close-range hit. Names differ among translations and games.
- **Galick Gun — Vegeta; see [Vegeta's signature techniques](#vegetas-signature-techniques).** Vegeta's beam recurs in several battles and adaptations.
- **Solar Flare — see [Foundational martial-arts techniques](#foundational-martial-arts-techniques).** The technique is useful to heroes and can appear in different fighters' repertoires.
- **Cell's Solar Kamehameha — Cell.** Cell combines a large Kamehameha-style beam with his regenerated, powered-up state. **Visual shorthand:** a large beam is charged and fired in a direct contest, emphasizing his ability to imitate techniques.
- **Perfect Shot / Cell's energy attacks — Cell.** Cell uses beams, explosive spheres, and copied moves. **Visual shorthand:** attacks can shift from precise shots to broad blasts as he changes tactics. Many have descriptive or game-specific names rather than one fixed canonical label.
- **Buu's candy beam — Majin Buu.** Transforms a target into candy or another small object. **Visual shorthand:** a beam or ray touches the opponent, who changes form; Buu may then eat the result. The effect is magical and can be resisted or reversed in exceptional circumstances.
- **Human Extinction Attack — Super Buu.** A huge volley directed at Earth's population. **Visual shorthand:** Buu releases countless tracking energy shots that scatter across the planet. It is a mass-targeting attack rather than a duel technique.
- **Vanishing Ball — Kid Buu and related forms.** A destructive sphere launched at a planet or opponent. **Visual shorthand:** a compact orb expands into a large explosive mass as it travels.
- **Dodon Ray / Kamehameha / copied techniques — Cell and other imitators.** Some characters learn or reproduce moves they have witnessed. The animation signals the borrowed technique, but the user's strength, control, and intent change its effect.
- **Moro's energy absorption — Moro.** A magical ability that drains life energy from individuals or planets. **Visual shorthand:** targets weaken as energy streams toward Moro, who grows stronger. It is an ongoing drain, not a single beam attack.
- **Granolah's precision shots — Granolah.** Granolah uses exceptional eyesight and pinpoint ki control to target weak points. **Visual shorthand:** brief aiming motions are followed by tiny, accurate shots and sudden changes of position. His style emphasizes precision over huge explosions.
- **Broly's ki blasts and charged rushes — Broly.** Broly's fighting style combines rapidly increasing power with overwhelming physical attacks. **Visual shorthand:** a roar or surge of aura leads into a rush, heavy blows, and broad energy blasts. Specific named attacks vary between his older film version, *Dragon Ball Super: Broly*, and games.
- **Gamma Blaster / Super Hero android attacks — Gamma 1 and Gamma 2.** The Gammas use superhero poses, energy weapons, and coordinated strikes. **Visual shorthand:** deliberate comic-book-style stances transition into bright, direct shots and aerial charges. Names and choreography are film- and game-specific.

### Fusion, team, and divine techniques

- **Fusion Dance — Goten and Trunks, Goku and Vegeta, and others.** A synchronized pose combines two fighters into one. **Visual shorthand:** mirrored movements meet at a fingertip touch; a failed pose can produce an imperfect fusion. The standard fusion lasts temporarily and requires exact coordination.
- **Potara Fusion — Vegito and other pairs.** Divine earrings combine two users. **Visual shorthand:** the earrings are put on opposite ears, followed by a flash and the appearance of a fused fighter. Rules about duration and separation receive further explanation in *Super* and vary by circumstances.
- **Big Tree Cannon / Galick Kamehameha — fusion and game variants.** Games give fusions combined or newly named attacks. **Visual shorthand:** a dramatic pose leads into a beam, rush, or multi-hit combination. These names and exact moves are often game-specific, so do not treat every one as a manga/anime staple.
- **Final Kamehameha — Vegito, especially in games and adaptations.** A combined beam motif associated with the fused fighter. **Visual shorthand:** a charged, wide beam is released with a forward thrust. Exact naming and visual form vary across media.
- **Super Ghost Kamikaze Attack — Gotenks.** Gotenks creates mischievous ghost copies that explode on contact. **Visual shorthand:** small ghostly figures emerge, act independently, and detonate when touched or triggered. Their unpredictable movement is part of the technique's character.
- **Buu-Ball / rolling attacks — Majin Buu and forms.** Buu compresses or curls his body into a fast-moving mass. **Visual shorthand:** he becomes a bouncing or spinning projectile that crashes through obstacles. It exploits his elastic body rather than conventional ki shaping.
- **Ultra Instinct — Goku's divine martial state.** The body reacts without the usual conscious delay. **Visual shorthand:** calm posture and subtle movement evade attacks by narrow margins before precise counters; aura and hair presentation differ by state and adaptation. It is a technique/state of combat, not a conventional projectile.
- **Destruction energy / Hakai — Beerus and other Gods of Destruction.** Destruction power can erase matter or a target. **Visual shorthand:** a controlled sphere or aura of destructive energy consumes what it touches. Its rules and portrayal are continuity-specific.
- **Angelic movement and defensive mastery — Whis and Angels.** Angels demonstrate effortless evasion, movement, and control that mortals train to imitate. **Visual shorthand:** attacks miss by inches as the Angel shifts almost imperceptibly, then redirects the exchange without a large counterblast.
- **Universe 6/7 team combinations — tournament fighters.** Fighters coordinate feints, holds, and covering fire. **Visual shorthand:** one combatant creates an opening while another lands a strike or launches a beam. Teamwork can overcome a raw power gap, though tournaments and adaptations choreograph these sequences differently.

### Defensive, movement, and utility skills

- **Ki guard / aura shield:** A fighter reinforces their body with ki to reduce damage. **Visual shorthand:** an aura tightens around the user just before impact. It consumes energy and does not make the user invulnerable.
- **Ki barrier:** A shaped field blocks or pushes back attacks. **Visual shorthand:** a translucent sphere or shell flashes around the user, sometimes cracking under pressure. Androids and other fighters use different forms.
- **Flight (Bukū-jutsu):** Ki control enables sustained flight. **Visual shorthand:** the fighter rises smoothly, hovers, then accelerates into aerial combat. It is basic to many later battles but not universal in early story arcs.
- **Ki sensing and suppression:** Fighters can detect energy signatures or hide their own. **Visual shorthand:** a character pauses to sense a distant presence; suppression can make a powerful fighter difficult to locate. Scouters provide an external, fallible alternative.
- **Telepathy:** Certain characters communicate mentally across distance. **Visual shorthand:** a pause or brief visual cue accompanies an unseen message. It is generally used for information, not damage.
- **Body splitting / cloning:** Some fighters create duplicates or separate parts of their body. **Visual shorthand:** multiple copies attack from different directions, usually with trade-offs in strength or control. Rules vary by character and version.
- **Sealing and containment:** The Mafūba and similar methods win by trapping rather than overpowering an enemy. **Visual shorthand:** a force pulls the target into a prepared vessel or space. Preparation and accuracy are critical.
- **Instant movement and feints:** Instant Transmission, high-speed movement, and afterimages can change position faster than an opponent can respond. **Visual shorthand:** a sudden disappearance or misleading image creates a counterattack window; the underlying skills have different mechanics.

## Fusions and teamwork

Fusion combines fighters, but method and result matter. The **Metamoran Fusion Dance** requires synchronized movements and generally creates a temporary fused warrior; mistakes can produce a weak or comically altered result. **Potara earrings** offer another fusion method. Fused characters such as Gotenks and Vegito have their own names, personalities, strategies, and limits.

Teamwork is just as important as fusion. Goku and Piccolo cooperate against Raditz; the Z Fighters create openings against stronger enemies; Gohan's victory over Cell depends on help from others; and the Spirit Bomb relies on energy freely contributed by others. Many battles turn on distraction, rescue, or a moment of trust rather than a new transformation.

## Major characters

- **Son Goku / Kakarot:** Saiyan raised on Earth. Curious, cheerful, and motivated by challenging himself; often believes enemies can change.
- **Bulma:** Inventor whose technology and initiative repeatedly make the adventures possible.
- **Krillin:** Goku's friend and fellow martial artist; resourceful and brave even when facing much stronger foes.
- **Piccolo:** Begins as an enemy, becomes a rival and mentor, and grows into one of Earth's strongest protectors.
- **Son Gohan:** Goku and Chi-Chi's son. Gentle and scholarly, with immense power that emerges under pressure.
- **Vegeta:** Saiyan prince whose path from conquest and pride toward family and responsibility defines a central rivalry.
- **Chi-Chi:** Goku's wife and Gohan and Goten's mother; determined that her children receive an education.
- **Trunks and Goten:** The next generation of Saiyans; Future Trunks's warnings reshape history.
- **Androids 17 and 18:** Humans altered by Dr. Gero who ultimately choose lives and loyalties of their own.
- **Master Roshi:** Goku and Krillin's eccentric teacher, whose training emphasizes fundamentals and persistence.
- **Frieza:** Galactic tyrant whose empire and fear of Saiyan power shape the Namek conflict.
- **Beerus and Whis:** Universe 7's God of Destruction and Angel attendant, who introduce divine training and cosmic hierarchy.
- **Shenron and Porunga:** Dragons summoned by different Dragon Ball sets, each with distinct creators and rules.
- **Broly:** A Saiyan whose extraordinary power and difficult history are depicted differently in separate film continuities.

## Continuities and adaptations

- **Original manga:** The foundation, from Goku's childhood through the Buu story and his meeting with Uub.
- **Classic anime:** *Dragon Ball* and *Dragon Ball Z* adapt the manga and include anime-only material. *Dragon Ball Z Kai* is a later edit of *Z* with a more manga-focused pace.
- **Dragon Ball Super:** The TV anime, manga, and films share a broad era and core concepts but differ in details and event presentation.
- **Dragon Ball GT:** A sequel anime with its own continuity, not an adaptation of Toriyama's original manga sequel.
- **Films, specials, games, and spin-offs:** Many tell alternate, self-contained, or game-specific stories. Older films often do not fit neatly into the main timeline, and games deliberately combine eras and characters.

For lore comparisons, identify the particular manga, anime, film, or game. A move's name or animation in a game does not automatically establish it as a main-story technique.

## Themes and glossary

### Recurring themes

1. **Growth has no finish line:** Every victory opens another opportunity to learn.
2. **Rivals can change:** Piccolo and Vegeta show that choices can matter more than origins.
3. **Strength carries responsibility:** Power creates a duty to protect others and consider consequences.
4. **Mercy is a risk:** Goku's compassion can give enemies a chance to change, but does not erase the danger of that choice.
5. **The next generation matters:** Gohan, Trunks, Goten, and Uub inherit a world shaped by earlier choices.
6. **Adventure and humor endure:** Even as the stakes become cosmic, the series retains its playfulness and wonder.

### Quick glossary

- **Capsule Corporation:** Bulma's family company, famous for technology that stores objects in compact capsules.
- **Great Ape / Ōzaru:** A giant ape form traditionally associated with tailed Saiyans exposed to Blutz Waves.
- **Ki:** Life energy that trained fighters sense, control, and use for movement, defense, or attacks.
- **Namekian:** A species including Piccolo and Dragon Clan members connected to the creation of Dragon Balls.
- **Potara:** Divine earrings used for fusion.
- **Saiyan:** The warrior species to which Goku, Vegeta, and Broly belong.
- **Super Saiyan:** A Saiyan transformation first reached by Goku during the Namek conflict.
- **World Martial Arts Tournament:** A recurring competition marking major stages of Goku's life.

## Further reading and viewing

For the central story, read the original manga from Goku's childhood through the Uub epilogue, or watch *Dragon Ball* followed by *Dragon Ball Z* or *Dragon Ball Z Kai*. Explore *Dragon Ball Super* and its manga, anime, and films as related versions with differences. Watch *Dragon Ball GT*, older films, specials, and games as separate branches of the franchise.
